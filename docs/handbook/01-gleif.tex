\chapter{GLEIF}\label{ch:gleif}

\section{Introduction}

The Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation (GLEIF) publishes the reference data of every legal entity identifier (LEI) in two levels: Level 1 says who an entity is -- its legal name, its addresses, its legal form and jurisdiction, and the status of the entity and of its registration -- and Level 2 says who an entity is consolidated by or managed by, and since when. GLEIF publishes the whole of both three times a day as a \emph{golden copy}, and the entity master data of chapter \ref{ch:entitymasterdata} has always been built on it. What the golden copy does not say is what held before: it states every LEI as it stands, and a name, an address or a parent that has changed is gone from it. Module \emph{gleif} keeps that history in the Silver layer, in the same shape as the FIRDS history of chapter \ref{ch:firds}: every state a record held is one version, with the first and the last day it held.

\section{Data pipeline}

GLEIF keeps every publication since February 2018 downloadable as a full copy of some 3.5 million LEIs, and beside each recent one publishes \emph{delta files} of the records changed in the day before it, some 26,000 a day, for about two months back. The history is begun from the full copy of the first publication on or after a start date the user sets, which decides where a history begins and nothing after that. While no delta files exist it is carried forward one full copy per month, and once they do, one delta per day, at the hour of day the history was begun at, so that each delta covers exactly the day since the one before; a day whose publication is missing is bridged by that day's full copy. Once a week the full copy is read instead of the delta, because the deltas list the records whose update time changed and not every change: a relationship GLEIF drops can leave them without a deletion, and 570 of half a million had after a month of deltas. The files go through the landing zone into the Bronze layer, where each is one Parquet table holding the columns the history keeps under GLEIF's own names, every one as text.

A record has a new version when anything the history keeps about it changes, which is read off a hash of those columns. Two columns are kept but not hashed: the time of the last update, and GLEIF's conformity flag, its own verdict on a record's data quality, which it recomputes without the record being updated and which the deltas therefore do not carry. A version begins on the day of the record's last update, which GLEIF records for every record, and that day does not depend on how often the history was fed: a record that changed on the 3rd of a month begins its version on the 3rd whether the change was read the next morning from a delta or at the end of the month from a full copy. A monthly step therefore loses only the intermediate states of a record that changed more than once within the month, not the days of the changes it keeps. A change GLEIF made without the record being updated begins on the day of the publication it was first read in, and a second change on the day a version began replaces that version. A version ends the day before the one that follows it; a relationship a delta reports as deleted, or a full copy no longer lists, is closed the day before.

Each level is a Delta table, and a publication is applied to it in one merge whose commit names the publication, so that a level either holds a publication or does not and the last such commit says how far the history has been carried. The two levels are carried forward together, one publication at a time. The rules of the history are written as transformations of data frames independent of the storage, as the FIRDS rules are, and the merge is tested against them.

\section{Software architecture}

Module \emph{gleif} has four parts: \emph{landing} chooses the publications a history needs and downloads them, \emph{bronze} imports them, \emph{history} holds the rules, and \emph{silver} applies them to the Delta tables. One call carries both levels to GLEIF's latest publication, and serves the first run and every daily one alike:
\begin{quote}
\verb|gleif.update_gleif(spark, "paths.yaml", start_date="2025-01-01")|
\end{quote}
The entity master data set builds on it. Where the configuration file has a \verb|gleif| section and the history has been built, the names, countries and parents of the entities are read from it, as they stand or as they stood on a day, rather than from golden copies of the entity master data module's own; without it the entity master data module reads its own golden copies, as before.
